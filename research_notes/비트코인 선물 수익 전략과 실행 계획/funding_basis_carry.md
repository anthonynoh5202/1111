## Q1. Mechanics: spot + perp, spot + dated futures, cross-exchange spreads, COIN-M vs USDⓈ-M, and how Binance calculates funding

### Takeaway
All of these trades hold BTC (or BTC collateral) long and an equal-notional BTC short in derivatives, so price moves cancel. The trader earns one of three things: (a) the funding that longs pay shorts on perpetuals, (b) the fixed premium of a dated future over spot, which converges at expiry, or (c) the funding gap between two venues. On Binance, a "neutral" market pays shorts exactly +0.01% per 8h (≈10.95%/yr on notional) because of the clamp in the formula. BTCUSDT funding is capped at ±0.3% per settlement, and settlement switches to hourly once the cap is hit. Hyperliquid uses the same formula, pays hourly, and has a much looser 4%/hour cap.

### Cited Findings

**Binance USDⓈ-M funding formula and schedule**
- Formula given in Binance's 2025-09-18 announcement: Funding Rate (F) = Average Premium Index (P) + clamp(interest rate − P, 0.05%, −0.05%). For contracts with shorter settlement intervals the result is divided by 8/N, so a 4h interval halves it. — [Binance announcement 2025-09-18](https://www.binance.com/en-AE/support/announcement/detail/c00588a7e8504b3eb28d02a2da00530b) (snippet only; the exact "old vs new" change was not visible)
- Interest-rate component fixed at 0.01% per 8h interval (0.03%/day). — [Binance FAQ: Introduction to Binance Futures Funding Rates](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/360033525031) (snippet)
- Binance computes the premium index every 5 seconds and averages it, time-weighted, over the funding interval. — [Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/360033525031) (snippet)
- BTCUSDT cap/floor is ±0.3% per settlement and the default interval is 8h. If a contract reaches its cap or floor, Binance moves it from 8h/4h to hourly settlement, completing the change within about 15 minutes of the capped settlement. The announcement's illustrative example has BTCUSDT hitting −0.3% at 08:00 UTC on 2025-04-22, with hourly settlement starting at 09:00 UTC. — [Binance announcement 2025-05-02](https://www.binance.info/en/support/announcement/detail/3243c81a35bd4f0c86a37315c3dc96cc) (snippet)
- Third-party claim: for about 34 major contracts the cap is 0.75 × maintenance-margin ratio (= ±0.3% for BTCUSDT), other contracts use 2%, and the hourly-switch rule has applied since 2 May 2025. — [Coincub](https://coincub.com/blog/perpetual-futures-funding-rates-78-of-a-quarter-at-0-01/) (not verified against Binance)
- Whether and how hourly settlement reverts is **disputed**. One 2026 guide cites a rule (|funding| ≤ 0.025% for 16 consecutive cycles, effective 2026-01-02); another says Binance's documentation states hourly settlement is not reverted. — [Coinvado guide](https://coinvado.com/en/posts/binance-futures-funding-rate-guide-2026/), [FeeOtter guide](https://feeotter.com/en/articles/binance-futures-guide/) (search summary did not say which guide makes which claim). Binance did move five USDT contracts back to 4h in July 2025. — [Invezz, 2025-07-22](https://invezz.com/news/2025/07/22/binance-resumes-4-hour-funding-rate-settlement-for-five-usdt-contracts/)
- How often funding sits at the 0.01% clamp: Spark says Binance BTC funding is at 0.01% about 31% of the time and that Binance moved to dynamic hourly settlement in May 2025. — [Spark funding comparison](https://www.spark.money/tools/funding-rate-comparison). A Coincub piece headlined "78% of a Quarter at 0.01%" (venue and quarter unclear from the snippet). — [Coincub](https://coincub.com/blog/perpetual-futures-funding-rates-78-of-a-quarter-at-0-01/). **Conflict:** the two likely measure different venues or periods.
- BitMEX (Q3 2025 report, "The Anchor and the Ceiling"): average funding "now consistently hovers around a baseline of 0.01%/8-hour", and a significant amount of capital is deployed in basis trading to capture funding. — [BitMEX Q3 2025 Derivatives Report](https://blog.bitmex.com/2025q3-derivatives-report)
- Binance's own APR arithmetic: a constant funding rate of 0.09% per 3 days equals 10.95% APR. — [Binance blog: How to Use Binance Funding Rate Arbitrage Bot](https://www.binance.com/en/blog/all/how-to-use-binance-funding-rate-arbitrage-bot-3611863022773164727)

**Hyperliquid (DeFi perp DEX)**
- Same functional form: F = average premium + clamp(interest − premium, −0.0005, 0.0005). It is computed as an 8h rate but paid every hour at 1/8 of that rate. The premium is sampled every 5 seconds and averaged over the hour. Interest is 0.01% per 8h (0.00125%/h). Funding is capped at 4%/hour, "much less aggressive capping than CEX counterparts", and the cap and interval are the same for every asset. — [Hyperliquid docs: Funding](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/funding) (snippet); [QuickNode explainer](https://www.quicknode.com/blog/hyperliquid-funding-rates)
- HIP-3 (builder-deployed) markets use premium = 0.5 × (impact bid + impact ask)/oracle − 1. — [Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/funding)

**Dated futures (classic cash-and-carry)**
- Textbook trade: long spot $X and short a dated future $X, held to expiry or until the basis converges. The annualized basis for one contract is (F/S − 1) ÷ (days to expiry/365.25). — [Coin Metrics basis docs](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/market-data/market-data-overview/basis-metrics); [Sharpe.ai dated-basis doc](https://www.sharpe.ai/docs/arbitrage-dated-futures-basis)
- Binance quarterly futures are cash-settled and expire on the last Friday of each quarter (2020-era explainer; check current terms). — [Binance Square post](https://www.binance.com/en/square/post/42880). Binance publishes basis through the API endpoint GET /futures/data/basis (e.g., pair=BTCUSD). — [Binance API docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/coin-margined-futures/market-data/Basis)
- TradFi version: long a spot BTC ETF and short CME futures. CF Benchmarks' example shows a 1% monthly basis is about 12.2% annualized. — [CF Benchmarks](https://www.cfbenchmarks.com/blog/revisiting-the-bitcoin-basis-how-momentum-sentiment-impact-the-structural-drivers-of-basis-activity)

**COIN-M (inverse) vs USDⓈ-M (linear)**
- COIN-M margin and PnL are in BTC. One Binance BTCUSD COIN-M contract = 100 USD (ETH contract = 10 USD). — [Binance FAQ: USDⓈ-M vs COIN-M](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/85eac2bba0b342819122dc9bd4745e9b) (FAQ partly dated 2020); example: 100 contracts × 100 USD = $10,000. — [Cryptohopper](https://www.cryptohopper.com/news/three-benefits-of-coin-margined-perpetual-futures-that-you-need-to-know-774)
- Inverse contracts let BTC holders hedge without converting to stablecoins, and basis trades can be run by holding spot BTC and shorting an inverse perpetual. — [leverage.trading](https://leverage.trading/what-are-coin-m-futures/); [Spark glossary](https://www.spark.money/glossary/inverse-perpetual)
- BitMEX Q2 2026 report: collateral design alone produced a 3.93% annualized funding gap between the inverse XBTUSD and the linear XBTUSDT over a 3.5-year study ending Q2 2026. Q2 2026 was an outlier, with a net spread of +0.91% driven by April volatility. — [BitMEX Q2 2026 Derivatives Report](https://www.bitmex.com/blog/2026q2-derivatives-report) via [Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/bitmex-says-collateral-design-drove-3-93-funding-gap-that-traders-may-exploit-repeatedly/) (secondary; which side pays more was not stated in the snippets)
- Binance Multi-Assets Mode: BTC, ETH and BNB can serve as USDⓈ-M margin with a 5% haircut (0.95 collateral value) once moved into the USDⓈ-M Futures wallet. — [Binance FAQ: Multi-Assets Mode](https://www.binance.com/en-JP/support/faq/29b45c485d664028b9ca1cdf90b24f6f)

### Inferences
- **Why 0.01% is the "anchor":** with F = P + clamp(0.01% − P, ±0.05%), F equals exactly 0.01% whenever the average premium P is between −0.04% and +0.06% per 8h. Funding departs from 0.01% only when the perp trades well above or below the index. At the anchor a short collects 0.01% × 3 × 365 ≈ **10.95%/yr on notional** (simple). This is my derivation from the cited formula.
- Funding per settlement = position notional × rate (standard; consistent with Binance's 10.95% APR example). When BTCUSDT switches to hourly settlement after hitting the cap, the 8h-equivalent rate is split over hourly payments, so in an extreme regime cash flows arrive (or leave) every hour.
- **COIN-M "synthetic dollar":** hold Q BTC as COIN-M margin and short contracts worth Q × P₀ USD (Q × P₀/100 contracts). Inverse PnL in BTC is N × (1/P₁ − 1/P₀), so USD equity = P₁·Q + N − N·P₁/P₀ = Q·P₀. That value stays constant at any price, so a 1x inverse short needs no price-driven rebalancing and is very hard to liquidate. Funding arrives in BTC, which adds a small unhedged BTC exposure that should be swept periodically. (My derivation. A search summary claimed inverse hedges must be adjusted as price moves; that is true only when the short is not exactly 1x or when accrued funding/PnL is left unhedged.)
- **USDⓈ-M with spot held separately:** the short sits in the futures wallet backed by USDT while the BTC sits in the spot wallet. In a rally the short's loss draws down futures margin, and the spot gain does not count as margin. That is how "under-margined" short legs get liquidated even though the combined position is flat. Multi-Assets Mode (BTC in the futures wallet as collateral) or Portfolio Margin removes most of this mismatch.
- Approximate price rise that liquidates an isolated USDⓈ-M short with no top-ups: x = (1 + 1/L)/(1 + m), assuming a maintenance rate m ≈ 0.4% (assumption, not verified). That gives ≈ +99% at 1x, +49% at 2x, +33% at 3x, +20% at 5x and +9.6% at 10x (my arithmetic).
- Dated-futures carry locks a known return to expiry, so there is no exposure to funding flipping negative. It needs a roll at expiry, and on Binance COIN-M the locked basis is earned in BTC, so convert it to lock a USD return.

## Q2. Historical yields 2019–2026 (by year/regime, across exchanges), public proxies, compression, and current 2026 levels

### Takeaway
Carry was very high and erratic early on: funding of ±0.3% per 8h was routine in 2016–2018, Binance BTC funding averaged ~36% annualized in 2021, and the 3-month basis exceeded 40% in Q1 2021. It turned briefly negative in crises (FTX, November 2022) and spiked again in Q1 2024 around the spot ETF launches (Binance BTC funding above 100% annualized; CME basis at roughly 17–25% at times). It then compressed in 2025 to about the risk-free rate: Binance BTC funding averaged ~5.3% and the CME basis was ~4–5%, as ETF basis desks and Ethena (USDe peaked at ~$14.8B on 2025-10-04) arbitraged it. 2026 has been the weakest regime: funding was negative for a record 67 consecutive days from February to May, the CME basis was ~5% in January, leveraged funds turned net long CME by August, and sUSDe 30-day averages were ~3.5–4.9%, against a 3-month T-bill at 4.21% on 2026-11-02.

### Cited Findings

**Long-run and academic evidence**
- BIS Working Paper 1087, "Crypto carry" (Schmeling, Schrimpf, Todorov; April 2023, revised October 2025): the futures-spot basis ("crypto carry") is the return on a cash-and-carry trade. It "can reach exceptionally high levels, sometimes exceeding 40% per annum", and the BIS summary page cites up to 60% p.a. Drivers are demand from smaller trend-chasing investors seeking leverage, plus arbitrage capital limited by regulatory and margin frictions. Interest-rate differentials explain little. **High carry predicts future price crashes**, and rising carry coincides with costlier crash insurance. — [BIS WP 1087 page](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm); [PDF](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf); [CEPR DP 20719](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/20719.html)
- A search summary of the BIS paper says carry "averaged above 10% a year and reached up to 60%". — [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm) (secondary characterization; sample period not confirmed)
- He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures" (arXiv 2212.06888, v6 August 2024): deviations from no-arbitrage are larger than in FX markets, move together across cryptocurrencies, and **shrink as the market matures**. A simple strategy earns "high Sharpe ratios" even for investors paying Binance's highest trading costs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888) (exact Sharpe values not retrieved)
- BitMEX 9-year study of XBTUSD (May 2016 to May 2025, 9,941 funding periods): funding was positive 71.4% of the time. In 2016–2018 rates regularly exceeded ±0.3% per 8h (annualized above ±1000%). — [BitMEX Q2 2025 report](https://blog.bitmex.com/2025q2-derivatives-report). Press release: a "90% drop in extreme bitcoin perpetual futures funding rates since 2016". — [DL News / BitMEX release](https://www.dlnews.com/external/bitmex-study-reveals-90-drop-in-extreme-bitcoin-perpetual-futures-funding-rates-since-2016-signalling-market-maturation)

## Q3. Risks and real failure cases, and how professionals manage them

### Takeaway
Delta-neutral does not mean risk-free. The documented failure modes are: (1) long stretches of negative funding (67 days in 2026), (2) exchange insolvency trapping collateral (FTX 2022, e.g., Galois Capital), (3) auto-deleveraging (ADL) force-closing the profitable short leg in a crash, which leaves the trader long and unhedged (October 10–11, 2025 on CEXs and Hyperliquid), (4) collateral or stablecoin "depegs" on a single venue's price feed (USDe at ~$0.65 on Binance only), (5) liquidity and withdrawal bottlenecks between venues, and (6) liquidation of an under-margined short in a rally. Professionals mitigate these with off-exchange settlement custody, multiple venues, reserve funds, low leverage and margin buffers, unified (portfolio) margin, and fast capital rotation.

### Cited Findings

- 2026: a record 67-day negative-funding streak (K33, as of early May 2026). — [CoinDesk 2026-05-08](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/05/08/bitcoin-slips-to-usd79-000-doge-leads-majors-losses-as-negative-funding-rates-set-10-year-record); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/397531/bitcoin-breakout-odds-rise-negative-funding-streak-mirrors-bottoming-regimes-k33)

**How professionals manage these risks**
- **Off-exchange settlement:** Ethena says protocol backing is never deposited on exchanges. It sits with off-exchange settlement providers (Copper ClearLoop, Ceffu MirrorX for Binance, Fireblocks), several per exchange, so exposure to an exchange failure is limited to PnL accrued between settlement cycles (cadence cited as daily, 4-hourly or 2-hourly in different Ethena documents). — [Ethena: Exchange Failure Risk](https://docs.ethena.fi/solution-overview/risks/exchange-failure-risk); [Ethena: Custodial Risk](https://docs.ethena.fi/solution-overview/risks/custodial-risk); [Ceffu MirrorX](https://www.ceffu.com/en-AE/solutions/mirrorx); [Ceffu–Ethena](https://www.ceffu.com/blog/ethena-has-integrated-ceffu-mirrorx-off-exchange-settlement)

- **Buffers and execution checks:** Binance's funding-rate arbitrage bot reserves 10% of the initial investment as a margin buffer and only opens or closes when the futures–spot spread is within an acceptable range (typically 0.1%). — [Binance bot FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/detail/f330e17d6fc04679b9b21d6f9350e787)
- **Unified margin:** Binance Portfolio Margin uses a unified maintenance margin ratio (uniMMR). If adjusted equity falls below maintenance margin, the account risks liquidation. — [Binance PM info FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/how-to-view-portfolio-margin-information-from-your-binance-account-and-trading-interface-07bd62ee3fe24461a623da54fa0f2865)
- **Structural vs transient:** BitMEX recommends deciding whether a funding gap is structural or event-driven before allocating capital. — [BitMEX Q2 2026](https://www.bitmex.com/blog/2026q2-derivatives-report)
- **Leverage reduction after 10/10:** a source in The Block (October 2026) argues a crash of that size is less likely now because leverage is well below its October 2025 peak. — [The Block, 2026-10-06](https://www.theblock.co/newsletters/the-funding/2026-10-06-crypto-hedge-funds-performance-417863)

### Inferences
- **ADL is the specific nightmare for a retail spot-long/perp-short book.** In a crash the short is the profitable side, so it is the side ADL closes, at the moment the spot leg is falling and the trader is suddenly net long. Lower effective leverage on the short (more margin posted) moves it down the ADL queue. Holding the short on a deep BTC book (BTCUSDT on Binance) rather than thin alt books reduces but does not remove the risk.
- **Single-venue concentration:** a retail trader usually holds both legs plus all collateral at one exchange (e.g., Binance), so an exchange failure or freeze is a loss of principal, not just of the hedge. Off-exchange settlement (MirrorX/ClearLoop) is institutional-only in practice. The retail equivalents are keeping only the needed capital on the exchange and withdrawing profits regularly.
- **Collateral choice matters:** the October 10 depeg hit users whose margin was USDe/BNSOL/WBETH. Plain USDT/USDC or BTC as margin (or COIN-M with BTC) avoids the "exotic collateral repriced by an internal index" failure mode.
- **Rally risk:** at 3–5x on a USDⓈ-M short with the spot leg in another wallet, a +20–33% BTC rally (common in bull phases) can liquidate the short (see Q1 arithmetic), turning a "hedged" position into an outright spot long after a loss.
- **Operational errors** (wrong size or contract, a missed leg, forgetting that funding can flip to hourly settlement, API key compromise) are not documented in the sources found. For a beginner with an AI coding assistant they are probably the most frequent real-world risk.

### Gaps
- Whether Binance's BTCUSDT (as opposed to altcoin) positions were ADL'd on 2025-10-10 was not found.
- No documented retail case of a basis trader's short leg being liquidated in a rally was found.
- Withdrawal-freeze data for Binance specifically (e.g., during October 10) was not found.
- Ethena's April 2026 "rsETH incident" (possible collateral/backing event) was not researched because the search budget ran out.

## Q4. Implementation for an individual: Binance tools, fees, capital efficiency, minimum sizes, monitoring, alternatives, and Korea-specific access

### Takeaway
On Binance an individual can (a) use the built-in Funding Rate Arbitrage Bot or the Earn "Smart Arbitrage" product, (b) build it manually with spot BTC plus a USDⓈ-M short (ideally with BTC posted as Multi-Assets collateral at a 5% haircut), (c) use COIN-M (BTC margin, 100 USD contracts) as a "synthetic dollar" with no rebalancing, or (d) use Portfolio Margin if holding ≥100,000 USDT. Base futures fees are 0.02% maker / 0.05% taker (10% off with BNB) and the BTCUSDT minimum order is 100 USDT, so a few thousand dollars is mechanically enough. For a Korean resident the larger issues are legal and operational: domestic exchanges cannot offer derivatives, and Binance, OKX and Bybit are not FIU-registered (Google Play distribution of unregistered offshore apps has been restricted since 2026-01-28).

### Inferences
**Capital efficiency (yield on total capital C for funding F on notional N; my arithmetic):**

| Setup | Capital needed | Yield on capital |
|---|---|---|
| USDⓈ-M short, spot BTC in separate wallet, short at L× | N + N/L | F × L/(L+1): 1x→0.50F, 2x→0.67F, 3x→0.75F |
| USDⓈ-M short with BTC posted as Multi-Assets collateral (5% haircut) | ≈N (+ buffer) | ≈F |
| COIN-M 1x short against BTC margin (synthetic USD) | N | ≈F (paid in BTC) |
| Portfolio Margin (≥100k USDT) | ≈N (+ buffer) | ≈F |

**Round-trip cost (enter + exit both legs), VIP 0 with BNB:** spot 0.075% × 2 + futures taker 0.045% × 2 ≈ **0.24% of notional** (≈0.19% using maker orders on futures). The 0.10% spot base fee these figures assume was not confirmed in retrieved snippets. At the 0.01%/8h anchor this takes ~24 settlements (~8 days) to earn back; at 0.005% it takes ~16 days. Frequent rotation chasing spikes is therefore a losing game at retail fee tiers. The COIN-M route avoids the spot legs if the user already owns BTC.

**Suggested beginner path (inference, not advice):**
1. Paper or monitor first: pull Binance funding history and the basis endpoint through the API and log realized funding against a T-bill benchmark. This fits the repo's rules: LIVE trading mode is rejected by the project's config validator (CLAUDE.md), and AI analysis must use an API key or manual pasting, not app automation.
2. If going live, use the simplest robust structure: COIN-M 1x short against BTC margin, or USDⓈ-M with BTC in Multi-Assets mode at ≤1x. Avoid exotic collateral (USDe, BNSOL, WBETH) and leverage on the short.
3. Monitoring checklist: funding at each settlement (watch for the switch to hourly), 7-day average funding vs an exit threshold (e.g., below T-bill after costs), margin ratio or uniMMR, ADL lights, spot–perp spread at entry and exit (≤0.1%, as the Binance bot requires), BNB balance for discounts, exchange announcements (interval changes, delistings), and withdrawal status.
4. API security for a security professional: trade-only keys with withdrawals disabled, IP allow-listing, secrets read from files and never logged (as CLAUDE.md requires).
- The Binance bot is the lowest-effort option, but its displayed "APR" is a funding statistic, not realized net return. It does not remove exchange, ADL or negative-funding risk.
- For a Korean resident, the legal and operational overlay (unregistered offshore VASP, Google Play restrictions, Travel Rule withdrawals, overseas-account reporting) probably outweighs the mechanical difficulty. Hyperliquid avoids KYC but adds DeFi, bridge, ADL and regulatory uncertainty.

## Q5. Realistic net return for a retail investor in 2025–2026 conditions vs holding stablecoins in savings products

### Takeaway
Under 2025–2026 conditions, a careful retail implementation on Binance would probably have netted about **2–5% a year on capital** in a 2025-like year (Binance BTC funding averaged ~5.3%). That is roughly in line with or below the 3-month T-bill (4.21%, 2026-11-02) and Ethena sUSDe (~4.3–5.1%, Sep–Nov 2026), and it carries exchange, ADL and operational tail risk. Only a capital-efficient setup in a year when funding stays at the 0.01% anchor (~11%) clearly beats cash. 2026 has also contained a record 67-day negative stretch. Binance's flexible stablecoin Earn base rates are lower still (~1.5–2.5% plus short promotions).

## Q6. Tax and accounting complexity for a Korean individual

### Takeaway
As of 2026-11-07, Korea's virtual-asset income tax is still legally scheduled to start on 2027-01-01: 22% (20% national + 2% local) on annual gains above a ₩2.5M deduction, taxed separately as "other income" (기타소득), with no loss carryforward. On 2026-11-05, however, the government said it will seek a **further 2-year delay to 2029** together with a 3-year loss carryforward. The ruling party and government were to decide on 2026-11-08, and a bill would need to pass by the end of November. Separately, offshore exchange accounts such as Binance count toward the ₩500M overseas financial account reporting (해외금융계좌 신고) threshold. No source was found on how perp funding payments or futures PnL would be classified.

### Cited Findings
- Current law: income from transferring or lending virtual assets (양도·대여 소득) is 기타소득 taxed separately at 22% (20% + 2% local) on amounts above ₩2.5M. The base is total proceeds minus necessary expenses minus ₩2.5M, gains and losses are netted across coins within the year, and losses cannot be carried forward. — [Newsis, 2026-03-26](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20260326_0003565851); [Digital Daily, 2026-08-06](https://www.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2026080615003604537)
- Start 2027-01-01, with the first filing in the May 2028 global income tax return. — [Newsis, 2026-05-15](https://mobile.newsis.com/view/NISX20260515_0003631744)
- Acquisition cost (blog summary): moving-average method for exchange trades and FIFO otherwise. The deemed acquisition cost is the greater of actual cost and market price on 2026-12-31. — [WikiDocs blog, 2026-08](https://wikidocs.net/blog/@Insight_Lab/28203/) (not official; verify)
- 2026-11-05 government plan: delay to 2029, introduce a 3-year loss carryforward (losses realized before the start date excluded). The stated reason is that crypto tax-information exchange with the US begins in 2029, and taxing domestic users first would be unfair relative to offshore-exchange users. Both changes need National Assembly legislation. A public-private working group is to draft reforms by H1 2027 for the 2027 regular session, with detailed standards in H1 2028. — [Seoul Economic TV, 2026-11-06](https://www.sentv.co.kr/article/view/sentv202611060127); [Daily T](https://www.dailyt.co.kr/newsView/dlt202611060013); [Focus Economy](https://www.gungsireong.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=55205); [Nate News](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20261105n26259)
- Politics: Democratic Party finance-committee members recommended the delay to the leadership. A party-government-presidential office meeting (고위 당정청) on 2026-11-08 was to decide, with the bill needing passage by the end of November. The People Power Party has proposed abolishing the tax. — [Hankook Ilbo, 2026-11-03](https://www.hankookilbo.com/news/article/A2026110309020004613); [Hankook Ilbo, 2026-10-30](https://www.hankookilbo.com/news/article/A2026103017430004475). An earlier Upbit Datalab brief said the government was keeping 2027. — [Upbit Datalab](https://datalab.upbit.com/intelligence/ai-news-briefing/topics/11183)
- Overseas financial account reporting: reporting is required if the total of all overseas account balances (cash, stocks, bonds, **virtual assets**) exceeds ₩500M on any month-end in the year. Virtual assets have been included since the June 2023 filing, and Binance and Coinbase accounts are examples. Overseas wallet providers count as foreign financial companies, and accounts at bankrupt exchanges (e.g., FTX) are still reportable. Penalties reported as up to 10% or up to 20% (sources differ by violation type). The filing deadline is in June, and one 2026 report cites July 1. — [Blockmedia](https://www.blockmedia.co.kr/archives/321528); [Blockmedia (FTX case)](https://www.blockmedia.co.kr/archives/325432); [SBS Biz](https://biz.sbs.co.kr/amp/article/20000237616); [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2025053066687); [Economist (KR)](https://economist.co.kr/article/view/ecn202607150051)

### Inferences
- Until the tax starts (2027 or, if the delay passes, 2029), there is no virtual-asset gains tax in force. Records still matter now: from the start date, every spot sale, USDT↔KRW conversion and possibly each funding receipt could be a taxable event, and the deemed-acquisition-cost rule depends on year-end prices.
- Funding received in USDT (USDⓈ-M) or BTC (COIN-M), and realized futures PnL, have no clear classification in the sources found. They might be treated as virtual-asset income, as derivative-type income, or as something else. A Korean tax professional should be consulted. The government's own rationale shows authorities currently lack offshore-exchange data, but international exchange of that data (US from 2029) is coming, so a complete trade, funding and transfer log from the Binance API/CSV should be kept from day one.
- The ₩500M overseas-account threshold is reachable for larger basis books, because both legs and collateral at Binance count toward the month-end balance.
